## Made for you: your own radio stations

The Radio tab now starts with three stations made for you from what you play, keep in playlists
and give a thumbs up:

- **Your Radio** (named for you, like "Madison's Radio"): your favourites right now, the ones you
  loved a while back, songs from your playlists, and some you haven't heard yet from artists you
  love.
- **Your Rewind**: the songs you couldn't stop playing months ago, back on the air.
- **Your Discovery**: new to you, from the artists you play most.

They're real stations, not shuffled playlists. Each one is a single broadcast from your server, so
you tune in partway through a song, as you would on the radio. Songs crossfade into each other at
an even loudness. A programmer's clock keeps it varied: no artist twice in a row, and no song again
for hours.

Each station also has a DJ who knows your listening. It back-announces what just played, says
what's coming up, and introduces songs: one you've had on repeat, one you haven't played since
spring, one from your road-trip playlist. It gives the time on the hour, and it doesn't talk over
every song.

- **Web player and phone app:** the DJ speaks over the start of songs and the music dips under
  its voice. Settings › **The DJ talks** turns the voice off; its words still show.
- **Desktop app:** the DJ's words show in the player bar.

Skip moves the station on to its next song. A thumbs up plays a song more often; a thumbs down
means it's never played again. Now Playing shows what's coming up and what was played. In
Android Auto, your stations are at the top of **Radio**, and you can star them as favourites
anywhere.

## Security

This release also fixes the problems found in a security review of the server, the apps and the
release process. The most important:

- **Server requests:** the server no longer fetches addresses people give it on their behalf (a
  radio station's stream, someone's own source) when they point at cloud metadata, the server's
  own machine, or a home network.
- **Artwork:** the server only fetches artwork it handed out itself.
- **Relayed content:** only audio and video are passed through, and nothing it passes on can run
  in your browser.
- **Requests:** request sizes are capped.
- **Pasted lists:** a pasted wall of text can't keep the playlist importer busy.
- **Sign-in:** wrong passwords are now limited per account and address, so someone else can't
  lock you out, and a burst of guesses can't slip past the limit. Open connections close when you
  sign out elsewhere.
- **Phone app:** its sign-in is only ever sent to your server, over the same connection type.
  Speakers on your Wi-Fi never see it, and other apps on the phone can no longer browse your
  library through its media controls.
- **Desktop app:** its sign-in goes only to your server.
- **Release process:** the steps that build releases are pinned to exact versions and given only
  the permissions they need.

People's own sources can't be on the server's machine (`localhost`) unless the admin allows it
with `SAMM_LOCAL_SOURCES=true`; admins' sources can. Update the server first.
